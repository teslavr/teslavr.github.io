Tonebird

Ear training that starts by telling you where you stand. A free test scores your ear out of 100 and breaks it down — pitch, intervals, chords, and where notes sit inside a key — and then a course takes you from the first unit to seventh chords. Sing into the microphone and it tells you which note you hit. For iPhone and iPad.

Frequently asked questions

Why are the steps of a key numbered instead of named Do, Re, Mi?
Because Do means two different things depending on where you learned music. In the English-speaking world Do moves with the key, so Do is whatever note the key starts on. In Russia, Italy, Spain, France, Brazil and most of Latin America Do simply means the note C and never moves. Numbering the steps 1st to 7th means the same thing in both traditions, and most people recognise “the 5th” long before “Sol”.

I have never had a music lesson. Is this for me?
Yes — that is who the course is written for. Every unit starts with a short explanation and examples you can play, and answers carry a hint underneath: the song an interval sounds like, or what a chord feels like. The hints fade on their own once you stop needing them.

What is free and what is Pro?
The test is free and always will be, along with four trainers: higher or lower, the five core intervals, major or minor, and spotting a note that is out of tune. Pro unlocks the rest of the course and the trainers, including every singing exercise. It starts with a three-day free trial.

The microphone does not hear me.
Check that Tonebird has microphone access in Settings › Tonebird. Sing a vowel rather than humming — “ah” or “oh” works better than a closed mouth. Any octave counts: a bass and a soprano are both right.

My score went down.
The test draws new questions every time and the harder ones count for more, so the number moves a little between attempts. What matters is the trend over several goes, which the Progress tab draws for you.

How do I cancel the subscription?
On your device: Settings › your name › Subscriptions › Tonebird. Cancelling before the trial ends costs nothing. The free trainers stay free either way.

Do I need headphones?
Not for most of it, but they help a great deal with the tuning exercises, where the difference can be a twelfth of a semitone. For the singing exercises headphones also stop the app hearing its own notes.

Does it work offline?
Yes. Every note is synthesised on the device — the app ships with no audio files and makes no network requests at all.
